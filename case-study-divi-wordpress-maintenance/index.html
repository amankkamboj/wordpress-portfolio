The maintenance challenge

The agency reported an intermittently failing Divi Builder and needed help reviewing several existing WordPress sites. Upgrading also required attention to legacy modules, live-page styling and multilingual navigation.

This was maintenance of existing implementations. I did not design or build the websites from scratch.

My role in the Divi upgrade

I investigated builder errors, reviewed compatibility and reported replacing legacy testimonial and layout components with native Divi-compatible sections. I also reported checking stored legacy module data when a compatibility warning persisted.

I initially proposed staging. One hosting plan did not provide it; another staging copy had asset-loading issues. The client approved a backup-and-rollback approach for production changes. The record does not establish zero downtime or a measured performance improvement.

Follow-up layout and multilingual repairs

Client review after the upgrades identified differences between editor and live-page styling, inconsistent Spanish headers and missing mobile navigation. I worked through these issues, shared a mobile preview and reported completion of that navigation work.

The client confirmed that many translation-related errors were gone, while still identifying the mobile menu as unfinished at that point. A later missing header required another repair. These revisions are part of the project story rather than evidence of a trouble-free initial rollout.

Plugin troubleshooting with a clear division of responsibility

For a reviews widget, I explained that its output came from a Trustindex shortcode rather than an editable Divi module. I repaired the generated CSS issue; the client explicitly confirmed that fix.

The client then rebuilt and reconnected the widget to its review source and confirmed that it was working. That reconnection was the client’s contribution. On a separate WPBakery-based site, I reviewed maintenance and updated available plugins; premium-plugin updates still required a licensing or theme-package decision.

Multilingual sitemap work

The engagement also included investigation of sitemap entries associated with Spanish translations that remained published after the English pages were drafted. I reported correcting the matching publishing states, rebuilding sitemap data and clearing cache.

This investigation has its own focused case study. The record supports a reported sitemap correction, without establishing improved Google rankings or removal from the search index.
